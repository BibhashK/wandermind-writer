Setup guide

Back to the app

Wandermind Writer researches a topic, writes an article, and puts it in your WordPress drafts. It needs three things from you: a key to write with, a key to research with, and permission to post to your site.

Your keys stay in your browser. They are sent with each request and used once, then discarded. They are never saved on our servers, and we have no database. Close the tab and they're gone.

Setup takes about ten minutes. You only do it once.

Get a Gemini key

This is the AI that writes your articles. It's free to start.

  1. Go to aistudio.google.com/apikey
  2. Sign in with your Google account
  3. Click Create API key
  4. Copy the key — it starts with AIza
Free tier limits. Google gives you a set number of free requests per day. If you write a lot, you may hit the ceiling and see a "quota exceeded" message. Waiting until tomorrow resets it, or you can add billing to your Google account to remove the limit — a full article costs a fraction of a cent.

Get a Tavily key

This searches the live web, so your articles are based on current news rather than the AI's stale memory.

  1. Go to tavily.com
  2. Sign up — the Google sign-in is quickest
  3. Your key is on the dashboard as soon as you're in
  4. Copy it — it starts with tvly-
The free plan includes 1,000 searches a month. One article uses about three, so that's roughly 300 articles.

Create a WordPress application password

This lets the app put drafts in your site. It is not your login password — it's a separate key you can revoke at any time without touching your account.

  1. Log in to your WordPress admin
  2. Go to Users → Profile
  3. Scroll to the very bottom, to Application Passwords
  4. Type a name, like writer, and click Add New Application Password
  5. Copy the password it shows you. It looks like abcd EFGH ijkl MNOP qrst UVWX
WordPress shows this password once. If you lose it, delete it and make a new one. That's normal and costs nothing.

Can't find the Application Passwords section?

This is the most common snag, and it's almost always one of two things.

A security plugin is hiding it. Wordfence, Solid Security, and others disable application passwords by default. In Wordfence: go to Wordfence → All Options, search the page for "application password", and untick Disable WordPress application passwords. Save, then reload your profile page.

Your site isn't on HTTPS. WordPress switches this feature off on any site that isn't secure. If your address bar shows "Not secure", ask your host to turn on SSL — most offer it free.

It still says "not allowed" when I connect

If the app reports that WordPress rejected your credentials even though the password looks right, a security plugin is likely blocking the connection itself. Installing the miniOrange WP REST API Authentication plugin resolves this on most sites.

Connect and write

  1. Open the app and click Settings
  2. Paste in your two keys, your site address, your WordPress username, and the application password
  3. Click Verify & Save. You should see "Connected as [your name]"
  4. Type a topic — or click Find trending topics and pick one
  5. Click Generate, and watch it work

When it's done you'll have an article on the left, an SEO score in the middle, and a LinkedIn post on the right. Edit anything you like, then click Push draft to WordPress.

It saves a draft, never a published post. Nothing goes live on your site until you read it and press publish yourself.

Common questions

Where are my keys stored?

In your browser, on your machine — they stay there between visits so you don't have to re-enter them. They travel to our server with each request, get used once, and are discarded. We have no database and keep no copy of them.

If you're on a shared or public computer, use the Clear my keys button in Settings when you're finished.

Fast or Quality — which should I pick?

Fast finishes in about twenty seconds and is good for most topics. Quality takes longer and uses a stronger model, which handles facts and nuance better. Use Quality when the topic is technical or the numbers matter.

The first load is slow.

The app sleeps when nobody's using it, and takes up to a minute to wake. Once it's awake it's quick. This is a limitation of our free hosting, not your connection.

It says all models are busy.

Either Google's servers are overloaded, or you've used your free daily quota. Wait a few minutes and try again. If it keeps happening, you've likely hit the daily limit — it resets tomorrow.

The article got a low SEO score. Is that bad?

Not necessarily. The score measures on-page basics — is the keyword in the title, is the article long enough, are there links. Every check tells you exactly what it looked at, so you can fix the ones you care about and ignore the ones you don't.

Can I edit the article before it goes to WordPress?

Yes. Both the article and the LinkedIn post are editable. Your changes are what gets sent. Read everything before you publish — the AI researches real sources, but you're the editor.

How do I revoke access later?

In WordPress, go to Users → Profile → Application Passwords and click Revoke. The app instantly loses the ability to post. Your Gemini and Tavily keys can be deleted from their own dashboards the same way.
